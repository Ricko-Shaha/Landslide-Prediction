This is a ranking, not a forecast. Every cell inside the district is scored on the same footing, so the colours say which ground is more susceptible than which. They do not say that a red cell has an 80 percent chance of failing: the inventory was built with equal numbers of landslide and non-landslide sites, so the model is calibrated to a 50/50 world rather than to real terrain, where landslides are rare. Elevation, slope and aspect are measured per cell; the remaining five factors are held fixed, so the pattern you see is terrain. The surface is interpolated between measured cell centres to read as a continuous field rather than a grid of tiles; that is drawing, not extra resolution, and the true spacing is the cell size given above. Nothing is painted outside the district, because outside it the model has no evidence.

The surveyor follows the rest of the page. Click anywhere on the live coordinate map and he walks to that point. Choose a surveyed site and he goes to ground in the same elevation and slope classes — inventory rows carry factor ratings and no coordinates, so there is no single place any of them came from.
Vertical scale is exaggerated 7×. The district is 210 km from end to end and 895 m at its highest, so at true scale there is nothing to walk over. Distances across the ground, and every number in the readout, are real.
These eight predictors are not measurements. They are reclassified factor weights: each terrain factor was binned into classes and each class given a score. Elevation takes only 4 distinct values across the whole inventory, aspect 8, and NDVI runs 0 to 36 rather than −1 to 1. A model trained on these cannot read a raw satellite reading, which is the single most important thing to know before extending this work.

Why accuracy did not choose

The original thesis ranked nine models on one train/test split. Repeat that split thirty times and every candidate above the baseline lands within about one standard deviation of the others. Taking the largest number would also have quietly priced a missed landslide and a false alarm the same.

The probability is not a risk

The inventory holds equal numbers of landslide and non-landslide sites, so the model is calibrated to a 50/50 world. Real hillsides fail far less often. Every number here ranks sites against each other and overstates absolute risk.
